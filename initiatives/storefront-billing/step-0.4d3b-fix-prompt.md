## Ruling 21 — RF-7: the focus hook goes (amends ruling 10, withdraws grant G-1 of ruling 2)

Round two measured what the first leg's `law` deviation suspected. ReactDOM 19.2.3 records the focused element before it mutates the DOM and focuses it again after the commit, before any layout effect (`react-dom-client.development.js`, `priorFocusedElem.focus()` at `:18068`, `PENDING_LAYOUT_PHASE` at `:18088`). In real Chromium 147, the real `DynamicListItem` in a keyed five-row list and a copy without the hook gave the same result in every case: Enter and Space, middle rows and both edges, repeated presses, a mouse click. The hook's own `focus()` call was a no-op each time. The edge defect of SB-82 came from the `disabled` attribute alone, and `aria-disabled` with the guarded `onClick` is its whole fix. The hook changes an outcome only when focus was NOT on the button at the commit (a programmatic click, a browser that does not focus a clicked button): there it pulls focus onto the move control, out of another row's text field if the caret was there. It also keeps its pending direction when a move handler does not re-render the row, and the next render then steals focus.

So ruling 10 now reads: `DynamicListItem` keeps its props, the control column rendered only when handlers are given, and `aria-disabled` with a guarded `onClick` and never the `disabled` attribute; focus after a move is React's own restoration, and there is no `useMoveFocus`. Grant G-1 is withdrawn: its two files are deleted.
