## Summary

`@@unique([parentSchemaId, order])` added to the `Schema` Prisma model + a `schemas_block_top_order` partial unique index appended to `prisma/sql/lms-checks.sql` — DB-level enforcement of `Schema`'s dual-scope positional-uniqueness invariant, the last Step 8 infrastructure sub-step before the 8.4 anchor. `Schema` has a dual scope: a top-level schema (`parentSchemaId IS NULL`) is positioned within its `Block`; a sub-schema (`parentSchemaId IS NOT NULL`) within its parent `Schema`. The invariant therefore needs **two** constraints with **two** mechanisms — Prisma `@@unique([parentSchemaId, order])` binds sub-schemas (Postgres NULLS DISTINCT means two top-level rows never collide under it, so it effectively binds only sub-schemas); the `schemas_block_top_order` partial index binds top-level schemas (`WHERE "parentSchemaId" IS NULL`). A flat `@@unique([blockId, order])` would be **wrong** — `blockId` is non-nullable on sub-schemas too, and two sub-schemas of different parents in one block legitimately share an `order`; a block-wide unique would reject them.

**The load-bearing finding — `lmsSchemaApi.reorder` was NOT rewritten.** The handoff brief's hypothesis (probably already two-pass — mirror 8.3.6's _outcome_, not 7.3.6's) was confirmed by the verbatim read: `schema/admin.ts:262-329` reorder was shipped two-pass in commit `0d7c6943` (subject literally "two-pass reorder"). The flavour-(h) `[[planner-mutation-invariant-trace]]` intra-tx trace ran upfront against **both** constraints and **both** scopes — Phase 1 stages every row to `-(i+1)`, Phase 2 to `(i+1)*10`; the negative staging space is disjoint from the positive operating space, so every intermediate UPDATE holds pairwise-distinct tuples; `blockId` / `parentSchemaId` are never mutated by reorder. The constraint pair is compatible with **zero** changes. `schema/admin.ts` is byte-identical after this step (D-8.3.7-3).
